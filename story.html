TutorVista — digital before digital was obvious

TutorVista was one of the earliest Indian consumer-internet companies to build its business on digital acquisition — and one of the first to reach an exit. Kedia joined as a trainee and took the marketing function over from a US agency, growing its scale and efficiency so sharply that it was brought in-house. Back in 2007, at 26, he was made head of marketing — at a time when Indian companies rarely handed a function that large to someone that young. Ability won over experience.

Flipkart — inference at scale

At Flipkart, leading digital marketing and pricing, we nearly doubled digital advertising spend while halving the cost of acquisition — within two months. Inside the company it was a seismic event. The lesson stuck: small improvements in inference and system design produce outsized effects at scale.

PhonePe — zero to 700 million users

We built PhonePe’s digital marketing engine from scratch. Within 15 months of launch, PhonePe crossed 100 million users — in 2017, a pace very few consumer apps anywhere in the world had matched. Then came the harder part: sustaining that run rate across hundreds of millions of users without giving up efficiency.

We did it against an entrenched incumbent and a global giant, using fewer resources, more efficiently. When we took over cashbacks from the business verticals, we cut that spend by 90% with negligible impact on revenue, while competitors kept spending. Today PhonePe has more than 700 million registered users.

We built a CRM engine that estimates the incremental revenue of every message for every user, running continuous test and control groups to measure cannibalisation, and machine learning to predict affinity. It maximised revenue while keeping the channel healthy — instead of spamming users into blindness. Later, running monetization, we saw from close quarters how growth is done across the country.

The pattern

When systems were built around narratives and dashboards, learning slowed. When they were rebuilt around first principles and causal feedback, outcomes changed dramatically.

Those step-changes weren’t signs of brilliance. They were signs that the previous system had been structurally misaligned with reality. That is the work we now do for a small number of founders.
